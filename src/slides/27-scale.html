Nobody outside the lab knows the closed numbers. Open-weight vendors publish theirs. The closed labs stopped: OpenAI’s last real number was GPT‑3’s and Google’s was PaLM’s, both superseded by 2023 releases that gave no figure at all — and Anthropic never published one. The closed-weight bars are inference rather than fact: the two published attempts to measure them from the outside disagree by roughly six times, which is why they are drawn as ranges. The lesson survives the uncertainty. Even the floor of the most conservative estimate is fifty times the largest model a laptop can hold, and over a thousand times the one you downloaded tonight. Note too that the dot on each bar is what actually runs per token: a sparse model holds the whole total in memory but reads only that slice for each word. Memory is set by the big number, speed by the small one — which is the one place section 03’s formula forks. Not tonight’s problem — everything on the download slide is dense, so the two numbers are the same — but it is why a sparse model can feel faster than its file size says it has any right to.
